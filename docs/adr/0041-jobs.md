# ADR 0041: Jobs of an environment, and a tenant's job that runs exactly once

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-18

A job is a container that runs to its end, with a deadline, an exit code
and a log; a run is one execution of it. An environment's job is
declared in its deployment: a deploy job runs before the deployment's
services move, once per template, and a manual job runs when asked; a
job runs once at a time in its environment, and a failed last attempt
fails the deployment. A tenant's job is made by one call that carries
its whole input, runs exactly once and is never retried; its exit, a
small result file and the tail of its log stay under its one ID for
seven days after its pod is gone. It belongs to an environment and
reaches what its create says (ADR 0035); a build is a tenant's job of
the builder image (ADR 0022).

## Context

ADR 0004 gives a pod a job that ends, and a deployment needs one for a
migration before its services move, or when a person asks. Work that has
no place in a deployment also needs one pod for minutes, with its input
in the call: an image build first, then a tenant's one-off maintenance.
FeCode built its builds in a sandbox driven by exec: a detached process
because the exec's process group is killed with the call, a poll of
`/proc` because `sleep infinity` reaps nothing, keep-awake calls against
the idle pause, the exit code, digest and log in files, the deadline and
the delete in the caller. Each step is a missing primitive, and a
sandbox's tokens and tools are open to a build that needs none. The
engine that runs an environment's job runs is already there: the pod is
an instance row that the sandboxes' engine places, admits, starts, moves
and deletes; what a job without a deployment needs is a place for its
template and a record that outlives its pod.

## Decision

### An environment's job

- A job of a deployment has an image, a command, an environment and
  references as a service has, a number of retries and a timeout, and a
  trigger: `on: deploy`, the default, or `on: manual`.
- A **deploy job** runs while its deployment is deploying, after the
  addons are provisioned and before the services move, when no run of
  its template has succeeded: a deployment that does not change a job
  does not run it again. Exit 0 succeeds; another code, no exit status,
  a container that does not start (with the node's reason), the timeout
  or a lost pod fail the attempt, which runs again while retries are
  left. A failed last attempt fails the deployment.
- A **manual job** runs when asked, as a new attempt of the target
  deployment's job. The environment's status shows a job's last run,
  and a run keeps a short tail of its log.
- A job runs **once at a time** in its environment, across deployments:
  a deployment waits for a run of the job that an older deployment or a
  person started, never cancels it, and takes its success under the same
  template as its own; the time it waited moves the start that its
  budgets count from (ADR 0018). A run is refused while another runs.

### A tenant's job

- A tenant's job is made by one call that carries all of its input: the
  image, pinned to its digest at the create (an image that does not
  resolve is the create's error, and no job is made); the command,
  replacing the image's; literal environment values and **secrets**,
  which reach the container as environment variables as well, sealed in
  the job's record, never returned, redacted from the stored tail and
  dropped when the job ends; image credentials (ADR 0022), sealed with
  them; its size; a **deadline**, counted from the create, the queue
  included, ten minutes when unset and at most two hours; a **result
  file** of the container's, read when the process has exited, at most
  4 KiB, returned as the job's result; labels; and the environment it
  belongs to and what of it it reaches (ADR 0035).
- A create carries an **idempotency key**, unique per tenant, as a tool
  call's (ADR 0023): a repeat with the same body answers with the job it
  made, another body is refused, so that a caller that lost the answer
  never starts a second build that pushes. The values of the secrets and
  image credentials are not part of the body's hash: a caller mints its
  tokens anew when it sends a create again.
- It runs **exactly once**: it has one run, so the job itself carries the
  phase, queued, running, succeeded, failed with its reason, deadline
  exceeded or cancelled, the exit code, the result and the tail of its
  log, under one ID, after its pod is gone. A lost node fails it, and it
  never runs again: it may have had effects, a pushed image, and only
  its caller knows whether a second one is safe. A container that does
  not start fails it too, with no exit status and the node's reason, as
  an exit would end it: it is not started again until its deadline. It never idles: it is
  not paused, suspended or hibernated for want of calls; a drain moves
  it as any pod, and its deadline counts on.
- Its pod goes as soon as the job has ended and its exit, result and
  tail are read: no ended pod holds memory (ADR 0004). The record stays
  **seven days** after the end; a caller that keeps a build's log copies
  it. A cancel ends a queued or running job as cancelled, its tail kept;
  a delete cancels a job that has not ended and removes its record.
- It is **not a sandbox**: no tokens, exec, tools, files, port forwards
  or previews, and absent from the tenant's sandboxes. It is not a new
  runner either: its pod is an instance row of the sandboxes' registry,
  as an environment job's run is, and the environments' controller ends
  it with the code that ends job runs. What differs is where the
  template comes from, the call and not a deployment, and where the
  record lives.
- The exit comes from the guest, which has a container's exit code and
  log (ADR 0004). Until the guest reports exits as events and ends the
  pod itself (ADR 0022), `ferund` reads them from the pod, which stays
  up after its container exits so that the result file and the tail can
  be read before it is deleted.

## Alternatives considered

- **A tenant's job as a run of an environment's job, with arguments per
  run.** It reuses the most and fits the least: the job's image and
  command are a deployment's, pinned, while a build's commit, tags and
  tokens are the call's; secrets per run would be a secret's new version
  and a deployment per build, a history of tokens; a job runs once at a
  time, a project builds three services at once; and the tenant would
  keep an environment for nothing but its builds.
- **A hidden environment per tenant for its jobs**, so that every query
  that knows instances knows them: one group for every job of the
  tenant, every FeCode project's builds reaching each other, and an
  environment without deployments that the controller would reconcile.
- **A sandbox driven by exec**, as FeCode built its builds: each step a
  missing primitive, and a sandbox's tokens and tools open to a build
  that needs none.
- **A builds API** in `ferund` (ADR 0022's draft): a job with a source
  fetched by `ferund`, which has no git client; the builder clones with
  a token its caller passes as a secret of the job.
- **A tenant's job retried by `ferund`** after a lost node or a failure,
  as an environment job's attempts are: it may have pushed, and its
  caller decides.
- **The ended pod kept** to read files or exec into: memory held until a
  delete (ADR 0004's alternative); the result file and the tail are
  what callers read.
- **A deadline from the start**, not the create: a job queued for want
  of capacity would wait without bound, and the caller would keep a
  second clock.
- **Exec into a running tenant's job** for debugging: a build's log
  makes it rare, and a job has no tokens to exec with.

## Consequences

- FeCode's builds stay builds in FeCode, and each is a tenant's job
  underneath: one create of the builder image with the commit and the
  repository as its environment, its two short-lived tokens as secrets,
  a result file for the digest and the build's ID as the idempotency
  key; its poller reads the job, and the result and tail once it has
  ended, and cancels it on a superseded commit (FeCode's ADR 0009).
- A tenant job's secrets reach the node's record of its pod as an
  environment's do (ADR 0044), for as long as the pod lives.
- A tenant's running jobs count against its quota (ADR 0031).
- A deployment whose job runs long waits for it, and so do its
  services; a job that a person started holds the next deployment at
  the same point.

## History

Environment jobs were decided with ADR 0018 on 2026-10-18 and built the
same day; a job running once at a time across deployments came on
2026-10-19 (d846a57, 23dbf00), with a deployment's budgets moved on by
the time it waited for another's run (1c72bb4). A tenant's job was
proposed as "a tenant's run" on 2026-10-30 (bc63348), built the same day
(2c58a0c) and renamed a job (86eff10); ADR 0035 (2026-11-07) put it into
an environment. Split out of ADR 0018 on 2026-11-08.
