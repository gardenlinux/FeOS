# ADR 0004: Pods are what feosd runs

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-10-14

A pod is a microVM that runs `feos-guest`, and one or more containers in
it; it is the only thing `feosd` runs, and FeOS has no VMs of its own. A
pod has no kind: sandboxes, services and jobs are the control plane's
objects, which it runs as pods that differ in their spec, not in anything
the node knows of them. Every pod has the same capabilities, reached
through `feosd`: its containers, images, tools, watches and port forwards
with the APIs they have in the guest, exec, credential offloading,
suspend and resume. The guest's API is a contract between `feosd` and
`feos-guest`, not a public one. How the guest supervises a pod's
containers is ADR 0048.

## Context

ADR 0001 makes the pod the unit of both products, agent sandboxes and
CaaS. An API that speaks VMs gives a client two APIs and two boundaries
across which versions must fit: it creates a VM, then talks to the guest's
API through the VM's vsock path. Raw VMs have no user of their own
(ADR 0005): a VM exists to isolate containers.

The products use a pod differently, and on the node what tells them apart
is what a process's exit means:

- A **sandbox** holds one long-lived container that an agent works in. The
  container runs nothing of its own; the work comes through exec and the
  tools, often for days, and no exit ends it. Processes the agent starts
  in the background outlive the exec that started them.
- A **service** runs what its image says, like a Kubernetes pod: the
  image's entrypoint and command, started again when it exits, checked for
  health, with traffic only to pods that are ready. Exec is for debugging.
- A **job** is done when its process exits, successfully or not.

A pod may hold several containers, each of which can exit on its own: a
service with a sidecar, a sandbox with a database beside it, a job with a
proxy beside it, and containers that prepare the pod before the others
start. Every container has a PID namespace of its own, and its process is
PID 1 in it, as with Docker.

What one product needs, the other needs too: a service that deploys over
SSH or talks to a Kubernetes API needs the access a sandbox's agent needs,
and an operator debugging a service needs the exec a sandbox lives on.

## Decision

### The pod

- A pod is a microVM that runs `feos-guest`, and one or more containers in
  it. An isolated container is a pod with one container.
- A pod is not a sandbox or a service: those are objects of the control
  plane's API, and it runs each as pods, one for a sandbox, one or more
  for a service. Their IDs go in the pod's labels, which `feosd` keeps and
  does not read. A pod has no kind, and `feosd` never asks what a pod is
  for.
- What one product needs is a capability of every pod: exec and the tools,
  watches, port forwards, credential offloading, suspend and resume. What
  makes a pod a sandbox or a service is the control plane's: which
  capabilities a tenant may call, the agent's tools mapped onto the pod's
  tool and exec calls, the policies that pause, hibernate and scale to
  zero. `feosd` reports what those policies need and decides none of it.

### The pod API

- `feos.pod.v1` is the node's API for pods. A pod is created with its
  spec under the ID the caller names (ADR 0019), so that a create
  delivered twice finds the pod it made; it is started, stopped, deleted,
  read, listed and watched for its changes of state; suspended, resumed,
  hibernated and restored (ADR 0008). Its microVM's serial console is
  reached as the pod's.
- The spec says what the containers run, the pod's resources as vCPUs and
  memory, never kernels or balloons, its volumes (ADR 0016), its network
  (ADR 0010) and its identity (ADR 0014). Its disk is the disk daemon's
  (ADR 0011), and its image comes as ADR 0022 says.
- The spec names the pod's identity, never its credentials: those reach
  the pod's `feos-authn`, which `feosd` runs beside the microVM, and the
  pod gets the access, never the key or the token (ADR 0014). Services use
  them as sandboxes do: a service that deploys over SSH or talks to a
  Kubernetes API gets the access. No client sees vsock ports.

### What a container runs

- A container's process is PID 1 of its container, as with Docker and
  Kubernetes.
- `command` replaces the image's entrypoint and command; the image's run
  when it is empty.
- A container's name is its ID in the guest, and its hostname.
- Processes started through exec belong to no container's lifecycle: their
  exits change nothing for the container or the pod (ADR 0023).
- A container keeps its writable layer across a stop and a start of its
  pod: a start boots the pod anew on its disk and adopts the containers
  with what they wrote.

### Exits, stops and the pod's state

- The guest records a container's exit code. The pod's record carries each
  container's state and last exit while the pod runs, and the pod's
  changes of state are streamed to whoever watches.
- A pod's state is its microVM's: pending while it comes up, running,
  stopped, suspended or hibernated, and failed when its bring-up failed or
  its microVM crashed. A container's exit changes the pod's state not at
  all; what follows from an exit is decided above the node (ADR 0018), and
  will be the guest's (ADR 0048).
- Stopping a pod sends its running containers `SIGTERM`, gives them a
  grace to exit and sends their last log lines on (ADR 0026) before the
  microVM shuts down, which ends whatever is left. A stopped pod's disk
  stays; a start boots it anew. Deleting a pod removes its microVM and its
  disk; its volumes stay (ADR 0016).

### A pod's services through feosd

A pod's containers, images, tools, watches and port forwards are reached
through `feosd`, with the APIs they have in the guest. A call that names a
pod in its metadata goes unchanged to that pod's guest over vsock; a call
without it is for `feosd`'s own services on the host. Any other service of
the guest is not served for pods.

### The guest's API is internal

The guest's API is a contract between `feosd` and `feos-guest`, not a
public one (ADR 0007). `feosd` is its only client.

### No VMs

FeOS runs microVMs as pods, for CaaS and agent sandboxes, and no VMs of
its own. There is no VM API: the microVM layer under the pods is internal
to `feosd` and served on no listener, and what operators had of VMs is a
pod's: its console, and its microVM in the pod's record. The layer keeps
only what pods use: no OCI images with a kernel of their own, no data
disks beyond the pod's disk and its volumes, no pause, no huge pages.

## Alternatives considered

- **VMs in the API.** Every client would have to know kernels, vsock and
  the guest's protocol, for VMs that have no use but to isolate
  containers.
- **Isolated single containers as the unit.** A pod with one container is
  that; a pod with several also carries sidecars and fits Kubernetes's
  model.
- **Two kinds on the node**, sandboxes and pods, or a kind in the pod's
  spec. `feosd` would know the products, and every capability would be
  decided once per kind. A sandbox with a database as a second container,
  or a service one execs into, fits neither kind.
- **The guest's services re-declared on the host**, as pod-scoped RPCs of
  their own. Every guest API would exist twice; forwarding by metadata
  keeps one.
- **Containers in the guest's PID namespace**, so that `feos-guest` reaps
  everything. Every container would see the guest's processes and the
  other containers'.

## Consequences

- Every client speaks pods, and nothing speaks VMs. A pod's microVM is an
  implementation detail of `feosd`, and so is the VMM (ADR 0005).
- The control plane maps its objects onto pods by their spec and labels
  alone, and reads nothing from the node about what a pod is for.
- Exec into a container whose process does not reap leaves zombies there,
  as with Docker and Kubernetes (ADR 0048).
- A CRI adapter that lets kubelet run pods on FeOS nodes would be within
  reach; it is not a goal (ADR 0001).
- The guest's APIs change only by addition (ADR 0007), since a suspended
  pod keeps the `feos-guest` it was suspended with.

## History

Decided on 2026-10-14 (f46c97f, c5853fb and f539c16), when the pod
replaced the VM in the API and sandboxes, services and jobs were put on one
kind of pod. The credentials the spec first named became `feos-authn`'s on
2026-10-15 (ADR 0014); the pod's ID became the control plane's to mint on
2026-10-26 (ADR 0019). `feosd` sends a stopped pod's containers `SIGTERM`
before the microVM shuts down since 2026-10-31 (6681651). The guest's
supervision of a pod's containers, decided here and given its order of
building by ADR 0022 on 2026-10-27, was split out into ADR 0048 on
2026-11-08 as the part not yet built.
